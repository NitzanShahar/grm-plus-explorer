The model in three steps

D-IRT (also written GRM+) keeps the graded response model’s item parameters and adds two person parameters.

1Graded response layer, with noise σ

P(y ≥ k) = logit−1( a (ψ − bk−1) / σ ),  k = 2,…,K

with P(y ≥ 1) = 1 and P(y ≥ K+1) = 0. Category probabilities are the differences between neighbouring cumulative probabilities (negatives clamped to 0, then renormalised). Dividing the logit by σ flattens every item for a noisy respondent and sharpens it for a consistent one; σ = 1 is the ordinary GRM.

2Reverse-keyed items

For an item worded against the trait, the probability vector from step 1 is reversed, so that its categories are again ordered from “disagree” to “agree” on the raw response scale.

3Choice layer, with acquiescence ζ

mk = log( PGRM(k) + 10−6 )
xk = 2(k − kmid) / (K − 1),  kmid = (K+1)/2
vk = mk + ζ · xk,  P(y = k) = softmax(v)k

The log-probabilities act as “choice values”. Acquiescence adds a linear tilt that runs from −ζ at the lowest option to +ζ at the highest. Because it is applied to the raw answer scale after step 2, it pulls toward agreement on every item, including reverse-keyed ones: ζ is not multiplied by the item’s direction.

How the parameters are estimated

Item parameters (a, b) are estimated first with a standard GRM (the R package mirt) and then held fixed. Each person’s ψ, σ and ζ are then estimated in Stan, with priors ψ ~ N(0, 1), ζ ~ N(0, 1) and σ ~ LogNormal(0, 1).

Things to try

  • Set ζ to +1.5 with no reverse-keyed items, then add some: the sum-score shift shrinks, but the item answers stay just as distorted.
  • Raise σ: extreme respondents drift toward the middle of the sum-score range and the correlation with the true trait drops.
  • Make σ small with a high a: the item behaves almost like a step function of ψ.
